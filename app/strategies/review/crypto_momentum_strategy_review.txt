Crypto Momentum Strategy Review / Handoff Notes
==============================================

File reviewed: CryptoMomentumStrategy
Strategy family: crypto_momentum
Profile: crypto_momentum.trend

Overall verdict
---------------
This is a sensible paper-test momentum signal file, not a proven money-making strategy yet.

The engineering structure is good. It is defensive, lane-specific, and suitable for paper testing. However, the strategy logic is still simple and the scoring formula is arbitrary until proven with backtesting and paper-trade data.

I would merge this for paper testing, but I would not trust it with live money until it has stats for win rate, average win/loss, drawdown, slippage impact, and whether the 1% stop is actually survivable in crypto.

What is good
------------
1. Crypto-only and fail-closed

The strategy checks the candidate asset_class and rejects anything that is not in the configured crypto profile asset classes. This is good because equity candidates cannot accidentally leak into the crypto lane.

2. Risk defaults are defensive

The stop loss is forced to be at least 1%:

    stop_loss_pct=max(float(config.crypto_momentum_stop_loss_pct), 0.01)

The target multiple is forced to be at least 1.5:

    target_multiple=max(float(config.crypto_momentum_target_multiple), 1.5)

That prevents accidentally tiny stops or bad config values from creating unsafe or nonsense signals.

3. Candidate gates are reasonable for a first version

The strategy requires:

- positive movement above a configured minimum
- enough discovery score
- enough trade count
- a valid close price

So it is not just buying any green candle.

Main concerns
-------------
1. The scoring formula is arbitrary

Current scoring formula:

    signal_score = round(
        min(
            100.0,
            (movement_pct * 135.0) + (discovery_score * 8.0) + (score_liquidity * 4.0),
        ),
        6,
    )

This may be fine for a first version, but the weights are basically assumptions until tested.

Important: confirm that movement_pct is always represented consistently. The comment says 0.15 means 0.15%, which is fine. But if another part of the pipeline ever sends 15 for 15%, the score will instantly cap at 100 and break the signal ranking.

Action:
- enforce movement_pct units clearly across the pipeline
- add tests around movement_pct scoring
- log representative score components during paper trading

2. It may buy late momentum

The strategy currently looks for crypto pairs that are already moving up with enough trade/discovery evidence. That is okay, but it may enter too late after a spike.

Missing protections:

- no max movement/spike filter
- no spread/slippage filter
- no minimum GBP volume/liquidity gate beyond trade_count/volume-derived liquidity score
- no candle structure confirmation
- no multi-window confirmation
- no reversal/overextension check
- no exclusion for extremely pumpy low-quality pairs

Recommended improvements
------------------------
Add extra profile parameters:

    "max_movement_pct": float(config.crypto_momentum_max_movement_pct),
    "min_volume_gbp": float(config.crypto_momentum_min_volume_gbp),
    "max_spread_pct": float(config.crypto_momentum_max_spread_pct),

Then add rejection gates before building the signal:

    max_movement_pct = float(profile.parameters["max_movement_pct"])
    if movement_pct > max_movement_pct:
        return None

    volume_gbp = to_float(candidate.get("volume_gbp")) or 0.0
    if volume_gbp < float(profile.parameters["min_volume_gbp"]):
        return None

    spread_pct = to_float(candidate.get("spread_pct"))
    if spread_pct is not None and spread_pct > float(profile.parameters["max_spread_pct"]):
        return None

Suggested default values for paper testing only:

    crypto_momentum_max_movement_pct = 2.5
    crypto_momentum_min_volume_gbp = 50000
    crypto_momentum_max_spread_pct = 0.25

These are not final trading values. They are just safer first-pass filters for paper testing.

Best next improvement
---------------------
Add rejection logging in paper/shadow mode.

At the moment, the strategy only emits passing signals. That means we cannot easily learn from near-misses or understand why candidates were rejected.

Add structured reject reasons such as:

    movement_below_min
    movement_above_max
    discovery_below_min
    trade_count_below_min
    missing_entry_price
    volume_below_min
    spread_above_max
    score_below_min

The goal is to later answer questions like:

- Are we rejecting too many good trades?
- Are thresholds too strict?
- Are we passing too many low-quality candidates?
- Is movement, discovery, or liquidity doing most of the filtering?

Example approach:

    def reject(reason: str) -> None:
        # In paper/shadow mode, log candidate symbol, strategy_id, reason, and key metrics.
        # Do not spam logs in live mode unless debug logging is enabled.
        logger.info(
            "crypto_momentum_reject",
            extra={
                "reason": reason,
                "symbol": candidate.get("symbol"),
                "movement_pct": movement_pct,
                "discovery_score": discovery_score,
                "trade_count": trade_count,
            },
        )

Acceptance criteria
-------------------
1. Strategy still only emits crypto signals.
2. Invalid/missing entry prices still hard reject.
3. Stop loss remains floored at 1% or higher.
4. New max movement, min volume GBP, and max spread gates are configurable.
5. Rejection reasons are logged in paper/shadow mode.
6. Unit tests cover:
   - rejects equity candidate
   - rejects movement below min
   - rejects movement above max
   - rejects low discovery score
   - rejects low trade count
   - rejects missing/zero close price
   - rejects low volume GBP
   - rejects excessive spread
   - emits valid crypto momentum signal
   - score calculation behaves correctly for movement_pct=0.15 meaning 0.15%

Blunt summary
-------------
Good engineering structure. Weak-but-valid first strategy logic.

This is fine for paper testing. It is not ready for live money until the strategy has real performance evidence and stronger filters against spikes, thin liquidity, and slippage.
